1. Scope and principles

WanderBunnies allows users to store and share trip information, documents, comments, messages, invitations, and other content. We aim to protect lawful expression and private collaboration while addressing illegal content, rights violations, abuse, security threats, and legal obligations.

This procedure is intended to support applicable consumer, privacy, safety, and Digital Services Act (DSA) obligations. It does not expand the legal scope of the DSA where the Service is exempt or subject only to different intermediary obligations.

2. Content that may be restricted

We may restrict or remove content that:

  • is illegal or facilitates illegal activity;
  • infringes copyright, trademark, privacy, confidentiality, or other rights;
  • contains threats, harassment, hate, targeted abuse, doxxing, or credible safety risks;
  • contains malware, phishing, fraud, spam, stolen credentials, or harmful code;
  • discloses unnecessary sensitive personal data, especially another person's data;
  • impersonates a person or organization or materially misleads others; or
  • breaches the Consumer Terms or creates a serious security, operational, or legal risk.

3. Notice and action reports

Anyone may report suspected illegal content or a Terms violation by emailing support@duerk.org with the subject line Content report, or by using [IN-APP REPORTING URL/CONTROL]. The report should include:

  1. the exact location of the content, such as a trip, message, account, or URL;
  2. a clear explanation of why it is illegal or violates the Terms;
  3. the legal ground or right allegedly affected, where relevant;
  4. the reporter's name and email, unless anonymous reporting is required for the issue; and
  5. a good-faith confirmation that the report is accurate.

We acknowledge reports where possible, assess them diligently and without arbitrary discrimination, preserve relevant evidence when necessary, and take proportionate action. Urgent threats to life, child safety, terrorism, or serious criminal conduct may be escalated to competent authorities.

4. Possible actions

Depending on severity, confidence, urgency, and legal requirements, we may:

  • take no action and explain why;
  • ask for more information or limit visibility while investigating;
  • remove or disable access to content;
  • limit features, group access, or messaging;
  • suspend or terminate an account; or
  • refer the matter to a rights holder, regulator, law enforcement, or emergency service.

We consider context, the content's likely harm, the person's rights, repeat conduct, evidence quality, and the least restrictive effective measure. We may act without advance notice when necessary to prevent imminent harm, preserve security, comply with law, or prevent evasion.

5. Reasons and user notice

When we restrict content or an account, we will provide a clear statement of reasons where legally and practically possible, including the action, main facts, applicable rule or legal basis, and available appeal route. We may withhold details that would reveal confidential detection methods, expose another person's data, compromise an investigation, or violate law.

8. False, abusive, or bad-faith reports

Reports must be truthful and specific. We may temporarily restrict reporting privileges or disregard repeated manifestly unfounded reports, while preserving access for urgent safety and rights matters.

10. Operational safeguards

Before relying on this procedure, the operator must designate trained reviewers, define escalation paths for imminent harm and child-safety reports, maintain an evidence and chain-of-custody process, record decisions and reasons, test response times, and ensure that privacy requests are not handled as ordinary content reports. Automated detection may assist review but must not be the sole basis for an adverse action where human review is required by law or the circumstances.
